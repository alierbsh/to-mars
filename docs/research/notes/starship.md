## Key Question 1 — Mass, dimensions and propulsion numbers per version (V1 / V2 / V3 / V4)

### Takeaway
SpaceX has published dimensions, propellant loads, thrust and engine counts for each version, but has **never published stage dry masses** — the dry-mass numbers in circulation are Musk 2021 targets or Wikipedia-derived values. The flying vehicle today is V3 (Block 3): 124.4 m stack, ~3,650 t booster propellant + ~1,600 t ship propellant, 33 Raptor 3 on the booster (nominal 250 tf each, 8,240 tf total) and 6 Raptor 3 on the ship (3 sea-level + 3 vacuum). A V4 (~142 m, 10,000 tf, 42 engines, ~200 t to LEO) has been announced by Musk for the 2027 timeframe.

**Other structural / staging facts**
- Hot-staging ring (vented interstage): ~1.8 m tall, added after Flight 1; during staging the booster shuts down all but its centre engines while the ship ignites and pushes off; Musk (2023) claimed ~10 % LEO payload gain; jettisoned after boostback from Booster 11 onward; on Block 3 it is **integrated into the methane tank and not jettisoned** (as of Dec 2025) — [WP-Starship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starship), [WP-SH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Super_Heavy). SpaceX's Dan Huot (Oct 2025): new boosters get "an integrated hot stage and more vent area" — [Space-F11](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/getting-even-bigger-whats-next-for-spacexs-starship-after-flight-11-success).
- On Flight 12 (V3) the booster executed a staggered shutdown "from 32 engines to five before stage separation" (Flight 1–11 boosters kept 3 centre engines) — [SFN-F12](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/05/27/faa-requires-spacex-led-mishap-investigation-before-resumption-of-starship-launches/).
- Grid fins: V1/V2 four electrically actuated steel fins, ~3 t each (Musk 2021); V3 three fins in a 90/90/180° arrangement, ~1.5× (SpaceX: "50 % larger") the earlier size, mounted lower, integrated with the tower catch pins — [WP-SH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Super_Heavy), [EDA-2021](https://everydayastronaut.com/starbase-tour-and-interview-with-elon-musk/), [Space-F11](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/getting-even-bigger-whats-next-for-spacexs-starship-after-flight-11-success).
- Musk 2021 component masses (V1-era): engines with mounts ~2 t each; booster tanks ~80 t; interstage ~20 t incl. 4 grid fins; propellant residuals ~20 t; "adding 1 tonne to the ship removes about 2 tonnes of payload" — [EDA-2021](https://everydayastronaut.com/starbase-tour-and-interview-with-elon-musk/) [OFFICIAL, 2021, pre-V2].
- V3 booster: substantially larger propellant transfer tube ("about the size of a Falcon 9"), two quick disconnects instead of one, metallic heat-shield tiles on the aft dome — [WP-SH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Super_Heavy), [Payload-V3](https://payloadspace.com/what-to-expect-from-starship-v3/).
- Booster burn time: 166 s (infobox) vs ~159 s (text); hot staging at ~64 km — [WP-SH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Super_Heavy). Flight timelines: MECO at T+2:18–2:37 depending on flight — [WP-F11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_11), [WP-F13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_13).

### Inferences
- **V3 propellant loads are internally consistent with SpaceX's relative figures only if V2 = 3,250 t booster / 1,500 t ship:** 3,250 + 400 = 3,650 t and 1,500 + 100 = 1,600 t exactly match the Wikipedia Block 3 table. So treat **3,650 t booster / 1,600 t ship** as the best V3 values (medium-high confidence); the often-quoted 3,400 t booster figure is probably a V1-era number. The frequently cited "1,550 t" V3 ship figure was **not** found in any fetched source.
- **Thrust:** 8,240 tf ≈ 33 × 250 tf, matching the "250 tf nominal" Raptor 3 figure. SpaceX's "+1,140 t vs V2" implies a V2 liftoff thrust of ~7,100 tf (not the 7,500 tf in the Wikipedia table), i.e. ~215 tf per Raptor 2 at liftoff — plausible for a de-rated nominal setting, but unconfirmed. For simulation, use 250 tf/engine SL nominal (8,240 tf stack), with 280 tf as the demonstrated maximum.
- **Isp interpretation:** the SpaceX 2024 infographic's 347/350 s values coincide with the Wikipedia stage infobox *vacuum* Isp of the sea-level engines (347 s Raptor 2, 350 s Raptor 3), while the sea-level Isp is listed as 327 s (Raptor 2) and 330 s (Raptor 3). The simulation should therefore use **~330 s SL / ~350 s vac for the sea-level Raptor 3 and ~380 s for RVac**, and treat the 350 s "sea-level" label in the Wikipedia Raptor table as a mislabel (inference).
- **Dry masses are the weakest numbers.** The Wikipedia V2 "85 t" ship dry mass is almost certainly an artefact of subtracting 1,500 t from a 1,585 t gross figure and contradicts Musk's own "~100 t" V1 target and the fact that V2 only lifted ~35 t to a suborbital trajectory; a realistic V2/V3 ship dry mass is more likely in the 120–160 t range (researcher's inference — no source). Booster dry mass: between Musk's 160–200 t target and Wikipedia's 275 t; V3 removes >1 t/engine of shielding (~33 t) but adds 1.3 m of tank and larger fins. Recommend bracketing the simulation with booster 200–275 t and ship 100–160 t and flagging both.
- Rough V3 liftoff mass check: 3,650 + 1,600 t propellant + ~250 t booster dry + ~140 t ship dry + ~37 t payload ≈ 5,700 t vs 8,240 tf → T/W ≈ 1.45 (inference; NSE's "~5,300 t" would give 1.55).

### Gaps
- No SpaceX-published dry mass for any stage/version; no V3 LOX/CH4 split (assume ~78 % LOX by mass per the V2 split and Musk's 2021 statement).
- Hot-staging ring mass not found in any source. V3 ship height not explicitly stated (implied 52.1 m).
- spacex.com/vehicles/starship (which lists current official specs) could not be read: the page is client-rendered JavaScript and returned only metadata to both WebFetch and curl.
- Raptor 3 RVac flight thrust not stated; the 275 tf "vacuum" figure (SpaceX, May 2026) appears to refer to the sea-level engine in vacuum.
- Could not confirm the origin of the Wikipedia Block 4 table values (81 m / 61 m / 4,050 t / 2,300 t / 2,700 tf / 200 t); they are consistent with Musk statements but should be treated as [SPECULATION] pending a primary source.

## Key Question 3 — Ship-to-ship orbital propellant transfer: demonstrated vs pending

### Takeaway
Only one propellant-transfer test has flown: an intra-vehicle header-to-main-tank LOX transfer on Flight 3 (14 Mar 2024, planned ≥10 t, NASA called it "successful by all accounts"). The ship-to-ship demonstration under NASA's 2020 Tipping Point contract has slipped from 2025 → "2026" → "NET late 2026" and, as of 11 Oct 2026, has still not been attempted; no depot or tanker has launched.

### Cited Findings
- Contract: SpaceX ran the Flight 3 demo "under a NASA contract awarded in 2020" (Tipping Point) — [EG-2024](https://executivegov.com/2024/04/spacex-conducts-capability-demo-as-part-of-in-space-refueling-technology-development/). (The widely reported $53.2 M value was not confirmed in any fetched page — see Gaps.)
- Flight 3 (14 Mar 2024): planned transfer of "at least 10 metric tons of liquid oxygen" from a header tank to the main tank; Amit Kshatriya (NASA Moon-to-Mars deputy AA), 26 Apr 2024: "successful by all accounts"; actual quantity moved not reported — [EG-2024](https://executivegov.com/2024/04/spacex-conducts-capability-demo-as-part-of-in-space-refueling-technology-development/). Aerospace America gives "5 t"; Wikipedia says "an unknown amount" — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/), [WP-PTD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_Propellant_Transfer_Demonstration). **Conflict: 5 t vs ≥10 t planned.**
- Planned demo architecture: first launch's ship becomes a depot (extra batteries, insulation and vacuum jacketing to limit boil-off); second launch 3–4 weeks later carries a tanker that docks; propellant moved by pressure differential; small thrusters settle propellant toward the outflow; ships undock, tanker reenters and is caught by the tower, depot stays in orbit; NET late 2026 from Starbase — [WP-PTD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_Propellant_Transfer_Demonstration). Wikipedia's launch list carries the pair as "2026 (TBA), Block 3 depot target + tanker chaser ~3–4 weeks later, customer NASA" — [WP-List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Starship_launches).
- Schedule slips: next step "scheduled for 2025" (NASA, Apr 2024) — [EG-2024](https://executivegov.com/2024/04/spacex-conducts-capability-demo-as-part-of-in-space-refueling-technology-development/); NASA Oct 2024: campaign to start ~March 2025 and finish over the summer with a design certification review — neither had occurred as of July 2026; "optimistically scheduled for 2026" — [WP-HLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_HLS); SpaceX's Dan Huot (Oct 2025): V3 docking adapters will be demonstrated "next year" (2026) — [Space-F11](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/getting-even-bigger-whats-next-for-spacexs-starship-after-flight-11-success); "After Starship's 13th test flight in July 2026, SpaceX still had not attempted it" — [SpaceDaily, 2026](https://spacedaily.com/t-starship-13-test-flights-orbital-refueling-mars-hurdle/); 11 Oct 2026: "orbital refueling has not been demonstrated" — [NSE-F15](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/10/11/starship-flight-15-will-attempt-to-recover-its-upper-stage-spacecraft-in-november-launch/).
- Hardware flown so far: V3 ships carry external docking adapters/ports and a redesigned quick disconnect; V3 booster has a Falcon-9-sized transfer tube and two QDs; insulated/vacuum-jacketed internal lines were introduced on S33 (first V2) — [WP-Starship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starship), [WP-PTD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_Propellant_Transfer_Demonstration), [Payload-V3](https://payloadspace.com/what-to-expect-from-starship-v3/).
- NASA HLS manager (Apr 2024): "We're going to have to do the [autonomous] prop transfer flights more than once or twice or three times" before a crewed flight — [WP-PTD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_Propellant_Transfer_Demonstration).
- Boil-off: William Notardonato (Eta Space CEO): losses could add "one or two more refueling missions" per ship; cryo models not validated with flight data — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/). NSE analysis uses a *hypothetical* 1 %/day (≤1 %/day "with shielding", unsourced), settling acceleration 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ g (generic figure), side-by-side docking as "prevailing consensus" — [NSE-Refuel](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2025/12/10/orbital-refueling-of-the-starship-architecture-operational-mechanics-feasibility-analysis-and-strategic-timeline/) [ESTIMATE/SPECULATION].
- Artemis III as restructured needs **no** in-orbit refueling; the HLS lunar landing (Artemis IV) does — [SPO-F14](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/starship-achieves-orbit-for-the-first-time/).

### Inferences
- With only one orbital Starship flight to date (28 Sep 2026) and Flight 15 (Nov 2026) devoted to catch attempts, a depot + tanker pair before year-end 2026 looks very unlikely; the realistic first ship-to-ship transfer is 2027 (researcher inference).
- No measured on-orbit boil-off number for Starship exists in any source; the simulation must treat boil-off rate as a free parameter (0.1–1 %/day bracket is reasonable but unsourced).

### Gaps
- Tipping Point contract value ($53.2 M) and the actual LOX mass transferred on Flight 3 not confirmed by fetched sources.
- No SpaceX statement of transfer mass, transfer rate, or settling thrust for the planned demo; vehicle serials for depot/tanker not announced.

## Key Question 5 — Heat shield, flaps, entry profile, landing burn, legs

### Takeaway
The ship uses ~18,000 hexagonal silica tiles rated to ~1,400 °C with an ablative backup layer (added after Flight 4), a felt "crunch wrap" between tiles (after Flight 10) and, on V3, improved tiles plus metallic tiles on the booster aft dome; Flight 10 flew an actively cooled tile experiment and deliberately removed tiles. Entry is a belly-first high-drag profile controlled by four flaps (V2 moved the forward flaps leeward at ~140°), ending with a flip and a 3→2→1-engine landing burn lasting ~18–20 s. SpaceX has published no peak-heating or Mars-entry numbers; the only Mars figure found is the generic ~7.5 km/s entry → ~1 km/s after aerobraking.

### Cited Findings
- Tiles: ~18,000 hexagonal black silica tiles on the windward side, rated to 1,400 °C (2,600 °F), pin-mounted with expansion gaps; heat shield + ablative layer = 10.5 t on a Block 1 ship (uncited in WP) — [WP-Starship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starship), [WP-Ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(spacecraft)).
- Ablative backup layer added under the tiles after Flight 4 (initially only on Flight 6's flaps), likely a carbon-composite-like "pyron" — [WP-Starship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starship). Felt "crunch wrap" between tile gaps added after Flight 10 — [WP-Starship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starship) [uncited].
- Flight 10 (Aug 2025): tiles intentionally removed from sections to test damage tolerance; "a new type of actively cooled tile" flown; results to be judged against "anticipated temperature margins" (no values) — [TC-F10](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/27/with-starship-flight-10-spacex-prioritized-resilience-over-perfection). Flight 11: tiles lacking an ablative backup removed as an experiment — [WP-F11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_11). Wind-tunnel tests on tile samples late 2024; Flight 10 "tested tile variants and stressed the rear flaps" — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/).
- V3: "improved tile design" on the ship; metallic heat-shield tiles on the booster's updated aft dome; most aft shielding removed — [WP-Starship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starship). Flight 12 used two Starlink satellites to image S39's heat shield in flight; Flight 13 used 6 camera-equipped satellites and a drone after splashdown — [NSE-V1V4](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/05/28/spacex-starship-from-v1-to-v4/), [WP-F13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_13).
- Tile production: Cape Canaveral facility 1,000 tiles/day (early 2026), design capacity 7,000/day, "enough for ~10 Starships a month" — [Payload-V3](https://payloadspace.com/what-to-expect-from-starship-v3/), [NSE-V3](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/04/16/detailed-review-of-starship-v3/).
- Musk, May 2025: "No one has ever developed a truly reusable orbital heat shield"; Mars' thin CO2 atmosphere yields more free oxygen on ionisation, which erodes tiles — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/).
- Flaps: four (two forward on the payload bay, two aft beside the engine bay/LOX tank), cameras in the hinges; V2 forward flaps thinner, moved leeward, angled ~140° (vs 180° for aft flaps and V1 forward flaps) to reduce pitch-up and heating; forward-flap damage on Flights 4, 5, 6 (Flight 4 nearly lost them) — [WP-Ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(spacecraft)). V3 flaps: SpaceX (Aug 2025) listed "flap upgrades" for Block 3 without specifics — [TC-F10](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/27/with-starship-flight-10-spacex-prioritized-resilience-over-perfection).
- Reentry damage history: Flight 10 survived an engine-compartment explosion at ~90 km (blocked chill lines) plus aft-flap inner-trailing-edge burn-through — [WP-F10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_10).
- Entry/landing timeline (Flight 13, V3): entry T+47:30; transonic 1:02:23; subsonic 1:03:01; landing flip 1:05:03; splashdown 1:05:21; engines 3→2→1 — [WP-F13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_13). Flight 11: entry 47:43; transonic 1:03:30; subsonic 1:03:52; landing burn 1:05:58; flip 1:06:00; 3→2 at 1:06:09; splashdown 1:06:25 — [WP-F11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_11). Flight 12: reentry 47:47 with aft-flap stress manoeuvre and dynamic banking; landing burn 1:05:06 with one engine disabled pre-ignition; splashdown 1:05:26 — [WP-F12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_12).
- Booster landing: 13 inner engines ignite ~6 min after launch, then all but 3 shut off before the catch; Flight 11 tested "configurations planned for future Block 3 boosters" — [WP-SH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Super_Heavy), [WP-F11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_11).
- Mars entry: "Aerobraking reduces speed from about 7.5 km/s to about 1 km/s" (article's description) — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/) [ESTIMATE].
- Landing legs: on Earth the ship is to be caught by the tower; "other bodies use landing legs"; HLS has landing legs, mid-body high-thrust GOX/GCH4 landing thrusters for the final "tens of metres"/within 100 m, body-mounted solar array, elevator, two airlocks — [WP-Ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(spacecraft)), [WP-HLSprog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Landing_System), [WP-HLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_HLS). Scott Hubbard: a 52 m tall slender lander is risky and there are no prepared pads on the Moon or Mars — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/).
- Ship RCS/attitude: cold-gas thrusters using ullage gas (four below the payload bay, two on the LOX tank, two header-tank vents near the nose); header tanks at the nose tip (LOX above CH4) feed the landing burn — [WP-Ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(spacecraft)).

### Inferences
- Landing-burn duration from the V3 timelines is ~18–20 s (flip at 1:05:03 → splashdown 1:05:21 on Flight 13), with a 3→2→1 engine step-down; useful as a boundary condition for a landing-propellant estimate.
- Earth entry so far has been from suborbital (~190–195 km apogee, ~orbital speed) or one orbit; no Starship has entered at lunar- or Mars-return speeds, and no Mars-atmosphere entry data exist. Any Mars entry model (7.5 km/s, CO2 chemistry, 100–200 t landed mass) is extrapolation.

### Gaps
- No SpaceX-published peak heat flux, peak temperature margin, entry angle-of-attack, or Mars entry velocity was found (the 7.5 km/s figure is an article's description, not a SpaceX statement). The oft-cited SpaceX Mars-page phrasing could not be read (JS page).
- Metallic tile tests on the *ship* (as opposed to the V3 booster aft dome) could not be confirmed from fetched sources; the Flight 10 "multiple metallic tile options" statement is **unverified** here.
- Mars landing-leg design: no source found beyond the HLS description. Tile thickness and individual tile mass not found.

## Key Question 7 — Known criticisms / open problems (brief, numbers only)

### Takeaway
Independent engineers' concerns cluster on refueling count and boil-off, reliability of a brand-new engine, landing a 50+ m vehicle on unprepared terrain, ISRU power, and the absence of validated long-duration life-support and radiation plans; several carry concrete numbers below. (Another researcher covers critiques in depth.)

### Cited Findings
- Refueling scale: 12 tankers per ship, 60 for five ships (Rapp); boil-off may add 1–2 tankers per ship (Notardonato); cryo models unvalidated in flight — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/).
- Reliability: Falcon reached ~150 flights before "no problems"; "Starship is nowhere near that" (Hubbard); 10 suborbital flights with 3 in-flight and 1 ground explosion by Aug 2025 — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/). Raptor 3 malfunctioned on all 3 V3 flights (Reuters/Sladen via Gizmodo) — [Gizmodo-F14](https://gizmodo.com/starship-has-finally-gone-orbital-now-comes-the-hard-part-2000818803).
- Landing: 200+ t landed mass is ~200× any previous Mars lander; 52 m tall slender vehicle on unprepared terrain (Hubbard) — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/).
- ISRU power: ~1 kW per kg O2 (Hecht); tens of thousands of m² of solar for SpaceX-scale production; no confirmed water (Rapp) — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/).
- Delta-v: Maiwald et al. (Scientific Reports, 2024) argue Starship has a delta-v deficit preventing a return flight (no number given in the summary) — [WP-Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Mars_colonization_program).
- Life support/radiation: as of 2019 SpaceX had not explained life-support or radiation-protection plans (The Verge); faster 3–4 month transits reduce exposure (Moses) — [WP-Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Mars_colonization_program), [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/).
- Schedule credibility: Zubrin offered a $1,000 bet against a 2026 landing and considers 2028 more feasible; Dreier (Planetary Society): "there's really no guarantee"; Lord Rees (2021): "dangerous delusion" — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/), [WP-Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Mars_colonization_program).
- Heat shield as unsolved: Musk himself (May 2025) — no truly reusable orbital heat shield has ever been built; CO2 atmosphere tile erosion — [AA-Mars](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/a-closer-look-at-spacexs-mars-plan/).

### Inferences
- The engine-reliability data point (Raptor 3 anomalies on 3/3 V3 flights, Flight 14's single-engine insertion) suggests engine-out tolerance is real but that nominal six-engine ship performance has not yet been demonstrated on V3.

### Gaps
- No fetched source gave numbers on dust effects, the lack of a crew abort system, or a quantified radiation dose for a Starship transit; these should be taken from the critiques researcher.

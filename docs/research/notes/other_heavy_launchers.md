# Other heavy-lift and super-heavy launch vehicles (excluding Starship and SLS) — state of play as of 12 October 2026

Scope: Blue Origin New Glenn (7x2 and 9x4), ULA Vulcan Centaur, SpaceX Falcon Heavy (Falcon 9 for reference), Ariane 6 (A62/A64), Rocket Lab Neutron, Long March 10/10A/10B, Long March 9, Long March 5, Angara A5 (+ Yenisei status), Stoke Nova, Relativity Terran R, Blue Moon MK1/MK2 (only as it bears on Mars-capable mass).

Confidence legend used throughout:
- **[H]** official/primary source (manufacturer page, agency statement) or several independent sources agreeing
- **[M]** Wikipedia or reputable secondary (NASASpaceflight, Spaceflight Now, Space.com) consistent with other data
- **[L]** single secondary source, internally inconsistent source, or figure the source itself flags as estimate
- **[U]** event or figure dated after June 2026 — reported by sources below but **unverified** per the assignment constraint (treat as "reported, not confirmed")
- **[calc]** my own arithmetic from cited numbers, not a sourced figure

Source publication dates are given in the source register at the end (last section). All units SI; where sources gave imperial units I converted and kept the original in parentheses.

## Key Question 1 — Dimensions and masses (height, diameter per stage, fairing, liftoff mass, stage dry/propellant masses, propellant types)

### Takeaway
Only Falcon Heavy/Falcon 9, Vulcan, Ariane 6, Long March 5, Long March 10 and the Chinese Long March 9 design have reasonably complete public stage-mass data; New Glenn's liftoff mass and stage masses are **not published** (only propellant tank volumes), and Neutron, Terran R, Angara A5 and Nova have partial data. A simulation will need to estimate dry/propellant masses for New Glenn, Neutron, Terran R and Angara from propellant volumes or analogues, and these estimates should be flagged.

### Inferences
- New Glenn GS1 propellant load can be *estimated* from the cited tank volumes: 850 m³ LOX × ~1,141 kg/m³ ≈ 970 t; 710 m³ LCH4 × ~422 kg/m³ ≈ 300 t → **≈1,270 t first-stage propellant [calc, low confidence]** (implies O/F ≈ 3.2). No source gives GS2 propellant or dry masses; total liftoff mass is therefore unknown but plausibly in the 1,400–1,600 t class given a 19.9 MN liftoff thrust (T/W ≈ 1.25–1.45) [calc].
- Vulcan's single liftoff mass (546.7 t) is inconsistent with the stage data: booster 382 t + Centaur V ~54 t propellant + dry + 6 SRBs (6 × 53 t = 318 t) exceeds 546.7 t, so the figure likely corresponds to a VC2 (two-SRB) build or is stale. Treat per-configuration liftoff mass as **[L]** and rebuild it from stage masses.
- Vulcan's Centaur V "CV-L" short variant (10.66 m) exists since Aug 2025; an enthusiast post describes it as the "85K" LEO-optimized Centaur (≈85,000 lb ≈ 38.6 t propellant) — single social-media source, **[L]**.

### Gaps
- New Glenn: no official liftoff mass, stage dry masses, GS2 propellant mass, or fairing length. Blue Origin's user guide is not public in the sources reached.
- Neutron, Terran R, Angara A5, Long March 10A/10B: stage dry and propellant masses not published in the sources reached.
- Long March 10 and Long March 5 fairing dimensions were not in the fetched pages.
- The NASA LSP site's "Vehicle Information" pages (which carry official NLS-contract dimensions) could not be queried (see KQ3 gaps).

## Key Question 2 — Propulsion (engines per stage, thrust SL/vac, Isp SL/vac, burn times, boosters)

### Takeaway
The propulsion data are the best-documented part of the dataset: Falcon Heavy, Vulcan, Ariane 6, Long March 5, Long March 10 and the Long March 9 design all have engine counts, thrust, Isp and burn times published. New Glenn's thrust was uprated in late 2025 (first stage 17.2 → 19.9 MN; upper stage 1.42 → 1.78 MN), and its sea-level/vacuum Isp are only available from secondary sources.

### Inferences
- New Glenn liftoff T/W cannot be computed without liftoff mass; the 19.9 MN figure applies to NG-3 (Apr 2026) and later vehicles; NG-1/NG-2 flew at ~17.1 MN [calc from NSF].
- For Vulcan, the per-engine RL10C-1-1 thrust (~106 kN) is well established in ULA material; the Wikipedia "203.6 kN each" should be read as a two-engine total.
- Long March 10's liftoff thrust (≈26.25 MN SL) exceeds Falcon Heavy's (22.8 MN) and New Glenn 7x2's (19.9 MN), making it the most powerful rocket in this set that has reached orbit in any form (the 10B single-stick variant, July 2026 [U]) — the full three-module CZ-10 has not yet flown.

### Gaps
- New Glenn: no official Isp (SL or vac) for BE-4 or BE-3U; no GS2 per-burn durations beyond NG-2 telemetry timings.
- Neutron and Terran R: no Isp or burn times published.
- Angara A5: no per-stage propellant masses; upper-stage data for DM-03 appear mis-copied in the source.
- Ariane 6 Vulcain 2.1 SL thrust/Isp and Vinci Isp not in the fetched page.

## Key Question 4 — Reusability and crew-rating status

### Takeaway
As of October 2026, only Falcon 9/Falcon Heavy (side boosters) and New Glenn (first stage, 2 landings in 3 attempts, one reflight) have demonstrated orbital-class booster recovery and reuse among these vehicles; China's Long March 10B reportedly achieved its first orbital booster recovery (wire/net catch at sea) on 10 July 2026 [U]. No vehicle in this set is crew-certified: Vulcan is "designed to be human-ratable" (Dream Chaser cargo demo pending), New Glenn is "engineered… to fly humans" and Blue Origin confirmed in Oct 2026 that an orbital crew vehicle is in work [U], and Long March 10/10A is designed crew-rated for Mengzhou with a max-Q abort test flown 11 Feb 2026 but no orbital Mengzhou flight yet.

## Key Question 5 — Flight history through 12 October 2026 and 2026 status (per-vehicle dossiers)

### Takeaway
2026 has been a hard year for the Western heavy lifters: New Glenn flew once (NG-3, 19 Apr, booster reuse success but payload lost) then lost its fourth vehicle and its only pad in a 28 May static-fire explosion (return to flight targeted Dec 2026 [U]); Vulcan flew once (USSF-87, 12 Feb, second SRB nozzle failure) and has been grounded from NSSL since, with a commercial return-to-flight NET 29 Oct 2026 [U]; Falcon Heavy flew twice (ViaSat-3 F3, 29 Apr; Roman, 30 Aug [U]); Ariane 6 flew four times including the first Ariane 64 (12 Feb) and first Block 2 (17 Jun); China flew the Long March 10A suborbital abort test (11 Feb) and the Long March 10B maiden orbital flight with booster recovery (10 Jul [U]); Neutron, Nova and Terran R have **not** flown.

## Key Question 6 — Crewed-Mars relevance (stated Mars architectures by these organizations)

### Takeaway
None of these organizations has published a crewed-Mars launch architecture built on these vehicles. China has the most explicit roadmap (CALT, June 2021): robotic sample return around 2028–2031 (Tianwen-3, two Long March 5s), crewed Mars launch windows 2033/2035/2037/2041, and a base; Long March 9 (44 t TMI, first flight 2033) is the implied carrier. Blue Origin has flown one Mars-bound payload (ESCAPADE, to an Earth loiter orbit) and announced the 9x4 super-heavy with lunar/deep-space marketing, but no Mars plan; ESA has booked the Mars Earth Return Orbiter on Ariane 64 (Sept 2027); Relativity is attached to a privately funded NASA Mars science mission (June 2026); Rocket Lab quotes 1.5 t to Mars for Neutron.

### Cited Findings
- **China (CALT, Wang Xiaojun, GLEX 14–17 Jun 2021)**: three-phase plan — (1) robotic technology preparation incl. uncrewed round-trip sample return ~2030 and site survey/ISRU; (2) crewed launches and Mars base; (3) Earth–Mars shuttle fleets. Crewed launch windows **2033, 2035, 2037, 2041 "and beyond"** (2043 not in this source). Nuclear propulsion "an important option"; a "Sky Ladder" transport system under study; vehicle types: carrier rockets, Earth-to-Mars shuttle rockets, Mars landing/ascent vehicles; no masses or rocket names given — [CGTN 26 Jun 2021](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2021-06-26/China-plans-to-launch-first-crewed-mission-to-Mars-in-2033-11oS5qxFp0k/index.html) [M].
- **China (CNSA, Li Guoping, IAC 15 Oct 2024)**: Tianwen-3 Mars sample return launches 2028 (two-launch architecture), samples back ~2031, ~25 kg orbiter + ~5 kg surface payload for international partners; Tianwen-4 Jupiter/Callisto ~2030; crewed lunar landing before 2030; ILRS in the 2030s — [Space.com 17 Oct 2024](https://www.space.com/china-exploration-roadmap-moon-mars-asteroids-jupiter) [M].
- **Tianwen-3**: two Long March 5 launches from Wenchang in the Dec 2028–Jan 2029 window (orbiter/Earth-returner on one; lander/ascent vehicle on the other); return by 2031; 51 candidate landing sites (Utopia/Chryse/Amazonis/Arabia); international payload call opened 11 Mar 2025 — [Wikipedia Tianwen-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-3) [M].
- **Long March 9**: "Crewed Mars missions have been proposed for the rocket" (no date); design TMI capacity 44 t; first flight 2033; also proposed for space-based solar power and data centers — [Wikipedia Long March 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_9) [M]/[L].
- **Long March 10**: designed for the crewed lunar program (Mengzhou + Lanyue), crewed landing targeted 2029–2030; no Mars role stated — [Wikipedia Long March 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10) [M]; [Xinhua 15 Aug 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202508/15/content_WS689eec3dc6d0868f4e8f4dcb.html) [H].
- **Blue Origin**: New Glenn page lists ESCAPADE (Mars solar-wind mission) as an award but states no Mars capability; 9x4 marketed for "megaconstellations, lunar and deep-space exploration, and national security (Golden Dome)"; Blue Ring Pathfinder flew on NG-1 and a Blue Ring is the planned RTF payload (Dec 2026 [U]); Blue Moon MK1/MK2 are lunar; no Mars architecture found — [Blue Origin](https://www.blueorigin.com/new-glenn) [H]; [Space.com 20 Nov 2025](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/jeff-bezos-blue-origin-will-build-a-super-heavy-version-of-its-powerful-new-glenn-rocket) [M]; [NSF 11 Oct 2026](https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2026/10/blue-origin-lc-36-rebuild-new-glenn/) [U] (article explicitly does not mention Mars).
- **ESCAPADE** (the only Mars payload flown by a vehicle in this set in 2025–26): 2 × 535 kg Rocket Lab Photon buses; ~1-year Earth-proximity loiter after 13 Nov 2025 launch; TMI burn Nov 2026; Mars arrival Sept 2027; science from spring 2028 — [UC Berkeley SSL](https://escapade.ssl.berkeley.edu/mission-design) [H]; [NSF](https://nasaspaceflight.com/2025/11/ng-2-escapade-launch/) [M].
- **Blue Moon MK2** (Mars-relevant mass class): >45 t launch mass, 16 t dry, 3 × BE-7, refueled in lunar orbit by Lockheed Martin's Cislunar Transporter (itself fueled by New Glenn upper stages); cargo variant 20 t reusable / 30 t one-way; Artemis V crewed target 2030, uncrewed demo 2027 (reports say MK2 unlikely before the 2030s; "Mark 1.5" crewed concept reported) — [Wikipedia Blue Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_(spacecraft)) [M].
- **ESA/Arianespace**: Mars Sample Return **Earth Return Orbiter** booked on Ariane 64, Sept 2027 (areocentric) — [Wikipedia Ariane launch list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ariane_launches_(2020%E2%80%932029)) [M]. ESA's Rosalind Franklin rover is booked on Falcon Heavy, Oct 2028 — [Wikipedia Falcon Heavy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_Heavy) [M].
- **Relativity**: June 2026 NASA public-private partnership for a privately funded Mars science mission (headline: NASA Mars orbiter) — [Wikipedia Relativity Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativity_Space) [M]; [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/ex-google-ceo-eric-schmidts-relativity-space-selected-for-upcoming-nasa-mars-orbiter-mission/) (title only).
- **Rocket Lab**: Neutron "claimed 1,500 kg to Mars and Venus"; design "meant to support deep space missions"; no mission named — [NSF 2 Sep 2026](https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2026/09/rocket-lab-update/) [U]; [Wikipedia Neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Lab_Neutron) [M].

### Inferences
- For a crewed-Mars simulation using non-Starship/non-SLS vehicles, the realistic 2030s candidates are Long March 9 (44 t TMI design, 2033 debut), New Glenn 9x4 (TMI unpublished; >20 t TLI; pad under construction), Falcon Heavy (16.8 t TMI, flying now) and Vulcan VC6 (~7.6 t TMI, secondary). Everything else is sub-10 t to Mars.
- Multi-launch assembly with hydrolox in-space stages (Blue Moon MK2 + Cislunar Transporter pattern; Long March 10's 3 × YF-75E third stage) is the only demonstrated-in-development path to Mars-class masses for these vehicles; none has stated Mars-specific refueling plans.

- No official Chinese document after 2021 restating the 2033 crewed-Mars date was found in the sources reached; the 2024 CNSA roadmap talk omitted crewed Mars. The "2043" endpoint appears only in a WION headline (not fetched).
- Blue Origin's Mars intentions (if any) for Blue Ring or New Glenn 9x4 could not be sourced; NSF's Oct 2026 article explicitly contains no Mars mention.
- Mass of Blue Ring Pathfinder and of the planned Dec 2026 Blue Ring payload: unknown.

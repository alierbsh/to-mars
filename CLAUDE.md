# Occupy Mars — working notes for Claude

- Vite 8 + React 19 + TS; three.js via @react-three/fiber + drei; zustand; vitest. `npm run build` runs `tsc -b` first — keep it type-clean.
- Physics lives in `src/sim/` and is pure TypeScript (no DOM) so it runs in the worker (`src/sim/worker.ts`) and in Node scripts/tests. Every number in `src/data/vehicles.ts` and `src/sim/*` should trace to `docs/research/` with a confidence tag (Y/O/D/T). Unpublished numbers become config sliders, not constants.
- Validation: `npm test` (anchor tests against NASA windows, USSA76, orbit propagation + mission-level expectations). `npx tsx scripts/mission-check.ts [vehicle] [preset] [window]` prints a full timeline; `scripts/ascent-check.ts`, `scripts/entry-calibration.ts` for the parts.
- Visual checks: build, then `node scripts/shot.mjs out.png W H waitMs "?vehicle=<id>&preset=<id>&window=<id>&t=<missionSeconds>[&still=1]" ["<selector>|<selector>…"]` (headless Chromium with software WebGL, pre-installed at /opt/pw-browsers). CSS animations/transitions do not advance there: `still=1` forces the lower third's opening animation to its end state, a `js:` prefix on a selector clicks through the DOM without actionability checks (the intro CTA needs it), `SHOT_PORT=<n>` lets shots run in parallel. `?model=<id>&view=side|nose|engines|mid&az=<deg>` shows a vehicle alone; `?terrain=mars|cape&pad=<padId>&alt=&az=&pitch=&x=&z=` the ground alone; `?wiki=<chapter>` opens the wiki. Atmosphere debugging: `?atmodebug=1|2` (in-scatter / transmittance only), `?hide=a,b` (scene parts), `?aerial=` and `?suni=` (strengths). Under SwiftShader the page is 10× slower than real hardware — don't tune timings to it.
- Places: `src/scene/capeSite.ts` (lat/lon helpers, pad ids/decks/mounds) + `pads.tsx` (procedural pad structures; the NASA Mobile Launcher GLB is Draco-compressed, decoder in `public/draco`) + `CapeCanaveral.tsx` (the painted tiles: cape 4096² / complex 2048² / pad 2048² with alpha water holes and a water colour map, scrub, the sea). The textures are painted offline by `scripts/cape-assets.py` (`region|cape|complex|pad`, `--only <id>`, `--n`) from hand-traced geography in the azimuthal-equidistant projection about LC-39A — the same parametrisation as `terrain.ts: capGeometry`, so x east / z south in metres of arc map 1:1. `region` needs the Natural Earth 10m GeoJSON (only raw.githubusercontent.com is reachable from the build container) and the Blue Marble 4k; big tiles are painted as 2×2 overlapping quadrants to stay under the memory limit. `src/scene/marsSite.ts` (DOM-free heightfields and per-pixel colour, shared with `marsMaps.worker.ts`) + `MarsSurface.tsx`. Terrain tiles are spherical caps built about the local origin; finer tiers match the coarser mesh at the seam and the coarser dips under them. Heightmaps are RG-encoded 16-bit PNGs from NASA-3D-Resources STLs (`scripts/stl2height.py` to regenerate).
- The climb: `src/scene/EarthBelow.tsx` puts the real globe (`earth-day-4k` + roughness), a 2000 km regional cap and a cloud deck (with shadows via `applyCloudShadow`) under the Cape tiles; `src/scene/atmosphere.ts` is the Rayleigh + Mie single-scattering model in metres, injected into built-in materials with `applyAerialPerspective(material, strength)` (per-vertex in-scatter/transmittance mixed in before tone mapping) and drawn as the sky by `skyScatterMaterial()` (BackSide, custom blending, alpha = 1 − T). Calibration lives in `ATMO` (sunI 10 against a directional light of 2.4, aerial 0.5); the sun direction is `SUN` in `AscentScene.tsx`. The ascent camera opens on the whole stack on its pad, the opening screens behind it too (`openShot` solves the distance and the aim from the lens so the stack fills the screen between the top bar and the lower third, `OPEN_CLEAR`; `OPEN_AZ` per pad — Falcon Heavy from the north-north-east, its strongback otherwise in front), eases to the climb's framing over 15 s, is horizon-aware (the dip angle from altitude), and all camera lerps are frame-rate independent (`1 − exp(−k·dt)`) — screenshots run at ~1 fps. The shown pitch turns at most `MAX_PITCH_RATE` (3°/s of mission time; the sim's upper-stage guidance steps ~30° at staging) and is published as `rigAttitude` for VehicleRig. Staging per vehicle lives in `STAGING` (`vehicles/index.tsx`, next to `ASCENT_ROLL`): Starship hot-stages (the ship's plume lit 0.6 s before separation with its `floors` override putting the splash tongues on the booster's vented ring) and the booster boosts back (keeps its own attitude, flips tail-first about `pivot` over ~6 s, lights at 0.4 throttle at `BOOSTBACK_IGNITION`, falls away by `stageDrop`; the camera pulls back to hold it for ~10 s — the stage-separation moment holds 1× for 10 s). Condensation collars (`Vapor.tsx`, open frustums at the shoulders: `STAGING[id].vapor` or the nose and the first interstage) follow the Mach number (0.88–1.35) below ~13 km. The Cape's land and sea tiles fade over ~4 km at their edges (`groundMaterial(…, 0.09)`, the `Water` mesh transparent at renderOrder 2): the regional tile draws the lagoons differently; the regional tile's beaches are a faint 12% line (`cape-assets.py region`).
- Flames: `src/scene/Plume.tsx` — a hull shaped in the vertex shader (sea-level column / vacuum bell by `expansion`), fbm noise + axial streaks + shock-diamond knots in the fragment shader, three layers (additive glow; body and core with normal blending, FrontSide only — double faces summed to a white tube). `LOOK` per propellant kind; `'plasma'` is the entry wake. The `floor` ref gives the deck distance for the liftoff splash sprites. Keep intensities in check: tone mapping is on.
- Orbit: `src/scene/Earth.tsx` (scene unit = 1 km; the Blue Marble 4k day/roughness maps from `cape-assets.py region`; the real cloud cover and the city lights from `scripts/orbit-earth-assets.py` (NASA, via the three-globe npm package) — lights tinted sodium orange and broken into districts close up by `fwidth`-faded noise; over Florida and the Bahamas the ascent's regional tile and its cumulus deck (`REGION`), whose shadows fall on it). The planet's spin and the clouds' drift are cosmetic, set by OrbitScene through `earthState`; `earthFixed(lat, lon)` is the Blue Marble's frame. The limb is a BackSide shell (`limbMaterial`: glow from each sight line's closest-approach height, two scale heights, ruddy at the terminator, the airglow's green line at 94 km) that must stay *below* the orbit radius (EARTH_R + ALT) or it fills the sky. Stars are points (`starField`), faded when the sunlit planet fills the frame. `OrbitScene` steers the cosmetic orbit and spin so each tanker docking and the departure burn are flown over the Bahamas in early afternoon (`SHOT_ANG`, `SHOT_LON`, `SHOT_SPIN`): the tanker closes over the last 1.5 days (the clock crawls there) and the camera dollies in on the pair; the burn has its own shot (`TMI_START` → `TMI_END`, or `TMI_END_TALL` on a portrait screen) while the clock holds at 1× (`hud.ts` moment `tmi`). The camera is smoothed in the ship's frame (`camRel`: the ship moves at km/s). Eclipse: the sun light fades and reddens with the grazing height of the ray to the sun; the environment (drei `Environment` + Lightformers: the planet below, black above) is turned to the ship's up and dimmed over the night side. Orbit plumes have no point lights (`lights={false}`: light falloff is in world units, km here) and vacuum plumes are faint (`vacAlpha` per propellant in `Plume.tsx`). Night side: lightning flickers in thick storm cloud (real-time, `cloudMaterial`); the tanker's thrusters puff through its final approach (`RcsPuffs`). `Earth` props: `sky` (stars, sun glow, Moon), `far` (a backdrop moved and scaled by its `root`: no depth written, transparent, drawn in order before everything; the sea glint softened by `roughMin`), `detail` (the regional tile and deck; off from afar, where its palette shows as a square). The limb shader works in its own frame (`uCam` set in `onBeforeRender`).
- Cruise: `CruiseScene.tsx` is one continuous shot from the ship — the owner's rule: the camera never leaves the rocket (people feel they are watching a video when it does); the bird's-eye view is `ui/CruiseInset.tsx`, a small SVG map in the event card (the Sun, both orbits, the arc flown and still to fly, the three bodies, the transit day, the light time to Earth; on a phone just the map at the strip's end). `CruiseGeometry` interpolates the transfer's samples (Catmull-Rom, heliocentric km) and turns heliocentric directions into the scene with `turn(f)`: the sun from the ship onto `SUN_DIR`, the ecliptic pole onto the scene's north; the stars turn with it. Next to a planet its hyperbola takes over (`sim/cruiseView.ts` — `departHyperbola`/`arriveHyperbola` and their hand-overs `departBlend`/`arriveBlend`, shared with the lower third's distances and the inset's light time; `src/sim/tests/cruiseView.test.ts` walks the hand-overs). Each body at its true angular size (brought in to `BACKDROP`, 20,000 km, and shrunk to match; drawn in order, no depth written), a point of light (`Dot`) once it is under ~5 px. The camera blends four framings (`Key`: the camera's turn, the ship's place on screen in degrees, its distance in ship lengths, the ship's turn): E looking back past the ship at Earth (the old departure shot); P the same turned a quarter round the sun's direction, ship and camera together, so Earth slides out of frame; M side-on to the ship, its nose toward Mars on screen (seen side-on the steel mirrors the sun behind the camera; from behind it is dark), the ecliptic north up; A behind the ship, the sun above, tilting down as Mars swings beneath (the old approach shot). The weights run in mission time and are eased in real time (`dtClock`, the clock's own cap); the ship's turn is blended as the camera sees it (`rel`), and the camera is placed exactly from the blend — only the viewer's drag is eased (a lagging camera let the ship drift on screen while the framing turned). `SpaceReflections` is captured again through its `api` when the nearer planet grows past a `REFL_LEVELS` step or swings 12° (the same map: no program changes). `EntryScene` opens where the cruise leaves the camera (`st.open`: behind and above the ship, then the fall's orbit). Mars is `MarsBody` (the global mosaic veiled by a storm, `marsLimb.ts`). `effectiveSpeed('cruise')` runs the clock at `CRUISE_NEAR.k` × the time from the nearer planet (≥ 180 s/s), so a planet shrinks or grows by the same share each second; `momentsAt` adds `depart`/`approach` with `rate: Infinity` — named by the lower third, not slowed.
- Entry and after: `EntryScene.tsx` holds the heat state (`st.heat`, tile emissive via `material.userData.heat`), the plasma wake and sparks, the scripted camera shots (glide / flip / descent / landed / steps / base); it reflects the canvas's own environment (no `Environment` of its own) and is lit by a sun and a hemisphere light. Above the dust (~85 km in a global storm) the sky is black and the limb glows (`marsLimb.ts`, in the ground group's frame, faded out below ~60 km) — the same look the approach shot ends on. A crewed Starship landing adds a `done` phase `First steps` (300 s, `sim/mission.ts`) animated by `scene/FirstSteps.tsx` as a pure function of phase time.

- Sound: `src/audio/sound.ts` synthesises everything with Web Audio (no samples); `AudioDriver.tsx` sets the mix every frame from the sim (thrust fraction, air density, camera distance, heat flux). It starts on the Start click; mute persists in localStorage.
- 3D models are procedural in `src/scene/vehicles/*.tsx`; stage groups are named by the sim's stage ids and carry `plume:<stageId>` anchors; `VehicleRig` animates them. `SPACECRAFT_GROUP/BASE/LENGTH` in `src/scene/vehicles/index.tsx` must match the model geometry.
- Mission time (s) is the playback axis; `effectiveSpeed` in the store paces each phase (ascent real time for 25 s then a ramp to the chosen speed; entry by altitude so the heating and the flip play slowly; landing and the first steps at 1×). On top of that `ui/hud.ts: momentsAt` lists the moments worth witnessing (staging, orbit insertion, the dockings, the TMI burn, peak heating, an ascent failure; the cruise's departure and approach are listed only to be named) and `playbackRate` drops the clock to real time around them — ramping in and out over ~1.2 s of real time, confined to the phases a moment's window touches — whatever speed was chosen; the lower third names the moment in rust by the clock.
- Performance: `src/scene/quality.ts` defines three tiers (high / medium / low: pixel ratio, MSAA, shadow map, logarithmic depth, sky samples, plume glow/tongues/octaves, sprite and mesh density, texture set, anisotropy, frame cap) picked by `detectQuality()` (`?quality=`, a saved choice, else the device) and stepped down by `AdaptiveQuality` in `Scene.tsx` when the opening screens or the first 40 s of the climb cannot hold 30 fps (one verdict per stretch; on the low tier a device that still cannot hold 25 renders fewer pixels, `DPR_STEPS` → `dprScale` in the store; the low tier also has a pixel budget, `maxPixels`, for tablets). The canvas runs `frameloop="never"`: `Pacer` calls `advance()` at `frameRate()` — uncapped while the probe measures, the tier's cap while playing, `fpsIdle` on the opening screens, 15 when paused and untouched, 4 under the wiki, the scoreboard or a phone's side panel — so the clock (`Playback`, a `useFrame` at priority −1), every scene's `useFrame` and the render all run at that rate, not at the display's 60–120 Hz; nothing else may call `requestAnimationFrame` for per-frame work. `mergeStatic` (`scene/mergeStatic.ts`) bakes each model's static meshes into one per material per named stage group (VehicleRig, the model viewer) and the pads' (their moving arms carry `userData.mergeRoot`); parts animated by name (`ANIMATED_PARTS`: leg, legExt, frost) and transparent materials stay separate; `?merge=0` compares. Never toggle a light's `visible` during a scene, nor that of a group holding one (it changes the light count and recompiles every material in the view — set its intensity to 0; `VehicleRig: showStage` hides a spent stage's parts but keeps its flame anchors), and set anything that changes a shader (fog, a map, the flames) in a layout effect, before the first frame — the R3F root commits at default priority, so a passive effect can run after a frame has been drawn without it (two compiles, one wasted); the low tier has no flame point lights (`plumeLights`) and coarser flame hulls (`plumeMesh`, `tongueMesh`). The audio context sleeps while muted or hidden. Mars's maps are prewarmed a few seconds after the climb is over (the DEMs, the site and its meshes are built once per session, `MarsSurface.tsx`; images read back through a canvas use `willReadFrequently` — a GPU canvas's getImageData waits for the GPU). Scene cuts do not compile: a program is keyed by the view's lights, fog and environment, so each view needs its own, and its first frame used to build them all (seconds on a phone). `scene/Prewarm.tsx` builds the next view (orbit → the cruise → the entry; `AFTER`) a few seconds ahead in a scene of its own that is never drawn — a portal with its own camera, inert inside (`warm.ts`: `useWarm()`; no camera moves, no `earthState`, no `Html`), the right fog and environment (`globalEnv`, captured from the canvas's `<Environment>`) — and `scene/precompile.ts` compiles it a few programs a frame against stand-ins of its lights, takes each linked program through its first use, uploads its maps and finally draws it once into a single pixel of the canvas before the frame that paints over it (`prerender`: the driver's pipeline state; stand-in casters switch the shadow pass through every depth-shader variant, and `primeLights` sets the render state's lights first — a render draws its shadows before it sets its lights up, so a view's first shadow pass would otherwise be keyed by the view before's lights; after each cut the canvas is primed the same way). The copy stays until the view cut to has been swept the same way (its hidden parts — flames, tanker, dust — then hold the programs), then goes; one job at a time, none while the probe measures or in the climb's first 40 s. Shared maps of a warm copy and the real view are reference-counted (`retainTextures`). `?prewarm=0` turns it off; `node scripts/stalls.mjs "<query with t= just before a cut>" [seconds]` (`STALL_SETTLE` ms before pressing play; long enough for the warm job to be built — minutes under SwiftShader) prints the long tasks at each cut (its first 2 s), in its first 10 s and over the shot, and the warm jobs' progress — at the orbit cut under SwiftShader 12.6 s of long tasks → 0.4 s; with the one-shot cruise, 0.19 s at the cut into it and 0.19 s at the entry. Consumers read `useQuality()` / `qualityNow()` (`scene/useQuality.ts`). The low tier has no logarithmic depth buffer (its gl_FragDepth write defeats early-Z), so the layered terrain tiers carry polygon offsets, one per layer from the top: the pad tile 0, the complex 1, the Cape 2, the sea 3, EarthBelow's regional tile 4, the globe 5 (two layers on one push fight; the sea had none and covered the land a few km up); the skies take their log depth from the vertex shader (`SKY_LOGDEPTH_*`) so the ground rejects their fragments. Half-size texture variants (`-half` suffix) come from `python3 -I scripts/texture-variants.py`, which also caps the masks; then `python3 -I scripts/ktx2.py --basisu <path>` writes the KTX2 (Basis Universal ETC1S, q255, mipmapped, flipped at encode, masks/roughness linear) next to every map — run both after repainting the Cape. The scenes load maps through `useMaps()` (`scene/maps.ts`): KTX2 first, transcoded by `public/basis/` (copied from three's examples) to the GPU's native block format, the original JPEG/PNG/WebP if that fails; `?ktx2=0` forces the originals. Pad scene GPU memory: high 300 → ~90 MB, low 99 → ~38 MB. The scrub reads the complex mask PNG on the CPU (`ImageLoader`), not the texture. basisu is built from github.com/BinomialLLC/basis_universal (`cmake -G Ninja .. && ninja basisu`). Textures are disposed when a scene unmounts. `?debug=1` logs draw calls, triangles and texture bytes once a second (`PerfProbe`). The lower third's clock is throttled to 12 Hz (`useThrottledT`); scenes read `t` from the store inside `useFrame`, never through React.
- Scoreboard: `scripts/scoreboard.ts` runs every rocket × assumption set × window (90 missions, ~20 s) into `src/data/scoreboard.json`; `ui/Scoreboard.tsx` (lazy-loaded, `?board=1`) shows it with a preset switch, and a cell calls `flyMission(cfg, 'scoreboard')` (the pick step, preselected). **Re-run the script after any change to the sim or the vehicle data** — `src/sim/tests/scoreboard.test.ts` re-runs a cell per rocket and fails when the JSON is stale. `cellConfig`/`cellLabel` live in `ui/scoreboardTypes.ts`.
- Sharing: `ui/share.ts` puts the settings that differ from `DEFAULT_CONFIG` into `?mission=<base64url JSON>` (validated key by key on the way in); such a link opens on the intro's pick step with the mission described (`pickFrom: 'shared'` in the store) and the param is dropped on launch. The Share button on the verdict card uses the share sheet on touch devices, the clipboard elsewhere, and a toast (`showToast`). - Languages: the site is in English, Türkçe, Español, Français, Deutsch, हिन्दी and 中文 (`src/i18n`). Timeline marks and the clock's moments are keyed by the sim's message keys (`hud.ts: MARK_OF`, `momentsAt`). After changing `en/sim.ts` wording, re-run `npx tsx scripts/scoreboard.ts`. Every language has its own address — `/en/ /tr/ /es/ /fr/ /de/ /hi/ /zh/` (`i18n/langs.ts`, DOM-free): the `lang-pages` plugin in `vite.config.ts` writes `dist/<lang>/index.html` with that language's `<html lang>`, title, description, link preview, canonical and hreflang alternates (from the import-free `<lang>/meta.ts`, which `ui.ts` carries as `meta`; its `siteName` is also the wordmark, `og:site_name`, the share sheet's title and the link preview's mark) and a modulepreload of its catalog chunk; index.html only has the `<!--lang-head-->` marker.

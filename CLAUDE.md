# Occupy Mars — working notes for Claude

- Vite 8 + React 19 + TS; three.js via @react-three/fiber + drei; zustand; vitest. `npm run build` runs `tsc -b` first — keep it type-clean.
- Physics lives in `src/sim/` and is pure TypeScript (no DOM) so it runs in the worker (`src/sim/worker.ts`) and in Node scripts/tests. Every number in `src/data/vehicles.ts` and `src/sim/*` should trace to `docs/research/` with a confidence tag (Y/O/D/T). Unpublished numbers become config sliders, not constants.
- Validation: `npm test` (anchor tests against NASA windows, USSA76, orbit propagation + mission-level expectations). `npx tsx scripts/mission-check.ts [vehicle] [preset] [window]` prints a full timeline; `scripts/ascent-check.ts`, `scripts/entry-calibration.ts` for the parts.
- Visual checks: build, then `node scripts/shot.mjs out.png W H waitMs "?vehicle=<id>&preset=<id>&window=<id>&t=<missionSeconds>[&still=1]" ["<selector>|<selector>…"]` (headless Chromium with software WebGL, pre-installed at /opt/pw-browsers). CSS animations/transitions do not advance there: `still=1` forces the lower third's opening animation to its end state, a `js:` prefix on a selector clicks through the DOM without actionability checks (the intro CTA needs it), `SHOT_PORT=<n>` lets shots run in parallel. `?model=<id>&view=side|nose|engines|mid&az=<deg>` shows a vehicle alone; `?terrain=mars|cape&pad=<padId>&alt=&az=&pitch=&x=&z=` the ground alone; `?wiki=<chapter>` opens the wiki. Atmosphere debugging: `?atmodebug=1|2` (in-scatter / transmittance only), `?hide=a,b` (scene parts), `?aerial=` and `?suni=` (strengths). Under SwiftShader the page is 10× slower than real hardware — don't tune timings to it.
- Places: `src/scene/capeSite.ts` (lat/lon helpers, pad ids/decks/mounds) + `pads.tsx` (procedural pad structures; the NASA Mobile Launcher GLB is Draco-compressed, decoder in `public/draco`) + `CapeCanaveral.tsx` (the painted tiles: cape 4096² / complex 2048² / pad 2048² with alpha water holes and a water colour map, scrub, the sea). The textures are painted offline by `scripts/cape-assets.py` (`region|cape|complex|pad`, `--only <id>`, `--n`) from hand-traced geography in the azimuthal-equidistant projection about LC-39A — the same parametrisation as `terrain.ts: capGeometry`, so x east / z south in metres of arc map 1:1. `region` needs the Natural Earth 10m GeoJSON (only raw.githubusercontent.com is reachable from the build container) and the Blue Marble 4k; big tiles are painted as 2×2 overlapping quadrants to stay under the memory limit. `src/scene/marsSite.ts` (DOM-free heightfields and per-pixel colour, shared with `marsMaps.worker.ts`) + `MarsSurface.tsx`. Terrain tiles are spherical caps built about the local origin; finer tiers match the coarser mesh at the seam and the coarser dips under them. Heightmaps are RG-encoded 16-bit PNGs from NASA-3D-Resources STLs (`scripts/stl2height.py` to regenerate).
- The climb: `src/scene/EarthBelow.tsx` puts the real globe (`earth-day-4k` + roughness), a 2000 km regional cap and a cloud deck (with shadows via `applyCloudShadow`) under the Cape tiles; `src/scene/atmosphere.ts` is the Rayleigh + Mie single-scattering model in metres, injected into built-in materials with `applyAerialPerspective(material, strength)` (per-vertex in-scatter/transmittance mixed in before tone mapping) and drawn as the sky by `skyScatterMaterial()` (BackSide, custom blending, alpha = 1 − T). Calibration lives in `ATMO` (sunI 10 against a directional light of 2.4, aerial 0.5); the sun direction is `SUN` in `AscentScene.tsx`. The ascent camera is horizon-aware (the dip angle from altitude) and all camera lerps are frame-rate independent (`1 − exp(−k·dt)`) — screenshots run at ~1 fps.
- Flames: `src/scene/Plume.tsx` — a hull shaped in the vertex shader (sea-level column / vacuum bell by `expansion`), fbm noise + axial streaks + shock-diamond knots in the fragment shader, three layers (additive glow; body and core with normal blending, FrontSide only — double faces summed to a white tube). `LOOK` per propellant kind; `'plasma'` is the entry wake. The `floor` ref gives the deck distance for the liftoff splash sprites. Keep intensities in check: tone mapping is on.
- Orbit: `src/scene/Earth.tsx` (scene unit = 1 km; the Blue Marble 4k day/roughness maps from `cape-assets.py region`, cloud/star maps in `public/assets/earth` built by `scripts/earth-assets.py` from NASA-3D-Resources). The limb is a BackSide shell (`limbMaterial`: glow from each sight line's closest-approach height, two scale heights) that must stay *below* the orbit radius (EARTH_R + ALT) or it fills the sky. `OrbitScene` gives each tanker docking its own shot: the tanker closes over the last 1.5 days (the clock crawls there), the cosmetic orbit eases round to early afternoon so the pair is sunlit, and the camera dollies in on the pair with the limb above.
- Entry and after: `EntryScene.tsx` holds the heat state (`st.heat`, tile emissive via `material.userData.heat`), the plasma wake and sparks, the scripted camera shots (glide / flip / descent / landed / steps / base) and a Mars `Environment` so the ship is lit from the sky. A crewed Starship landing adds a `done` phase `First steps` (300 s, `sim/mission.ts`) animated by `scene/FirstSteps.tsx` as a pure function of phase time.
- UI: the stage is the whole screen. `TopBar` (the "Occupy Mars Simulation" wordmark, the ship's badge + New mission → the intro's pick step, mute, Mission settings/Data/Wiki; the rocket is chosen only when a mission starts — no mid-flight switching), `VerdictCard` (≤4 stats), `Stage` (scene + the playback loop), `LowerThird` (dials, clock, accordion timeline with marks, engine grids, controls; `openSeq` in the store restarts its opening animation; marks come from `ui/hud.ts`), `MissionDrawer` (titled Mission settings; Graphics is its first section), `DetailsSheet`, `Wiki` (`src/wiki/chapters.ts`; a mark label opens its chapter via `chapterForMark`). Tracked uppercase `.label`s, glass panels, three text opacities — keep new UI in that register.
- Sound: `src/audio/sound.ts` synthesises everything with Web Audio (no samples); `AudioDriver.tsx` sets the mix every frame from the sim (thrust fraction, air density, camera distance, heat flux). It starts on the Start click; mute persists in localStorage.
- 3D models are procedural in `src/scene/vehicles/*.tsx`; stage groups are named by the sim's stage ids and carry `plume:<stageId>` anchors; `VehicleRig` animates them. `SPACECRAFT_GROUP/BASE/LENGTH` in `src/scene/vehicles/index.tsx` must match the model geometry.
- Mission time (s) is the playback axis; `effectiveSpeed` in the store paces each phase (ascent real time for 25 s then a ramp to the chosen speed; entry by altitude so the heating and the flip play slowly; landing and the first steps at 1×). On top of that `ui/hud.ts: momentsAt` lists the moments worth witnessing (staging, orbit insertion, the dockings, the TMI burn, peak heating, an ascent failure) and `playbackRate` drops the clock to real time around them — ramping in and out over ~1.2 s of real time, confined to the phases a moment's window touches — whatever speed was chosen; the lower third names the moment in rust by the clock.
- Performance: `src/scene/quality.ts` defines three tiers (high / medium / low: pixel ratio, MSAA, shadow map, logarithmic depth, sky samples, plume glow/tongues/octaves, sprite and mesh density, texture set, anisotropy, frame cap) picked by `detectQuality()` (`?quality=`, a saved choice, else the device) and stepped down by `AdaptiveQuality` in `Scene.tsx` when the opening screens or the first 40 s of the climb cannot hold 30 fps; `FrameCap` owns the render call (a render priority) and caps the rate, lower on the opening screens. Consumers read `useQuality()` / `qualityNow()` (`scene/useQuality.ts`). The low tier has no logarithmic depth buffer (its gl_FragDepth write defeats early-Z), so the layered terrain tiers carry polygon offsets; the skies take their log depth from the vertex shader (`SKY_LOGDEPTH_*`) so the ground rejects their fragments. Half-size texture variants (`-half` suffix) come from `python3 -I scripts/texture-variants.py`, which also caps the masks; then `python3 -I scripts/ktx2.py --basisu <path>` writes the KTX2 (Basis Universal ETC1S, q255, mipmapped, flipped at encode, masks/roughness linear) next to every map — run both after repainting the Cape. The scenes load maps through `useMaps()` (`scene/maps.ts`): KTX2 first, transcoded by `public/basis/` (copied from three's examples) to the GPU's native block format, the original JPEG/PNG/WebP if that fails; `?ktx2=0` forces the originals. Pad scene GPU memory: high 300 → ~90 MB, low 99 → ~38 MB. The scrub reads the complex mask PNG on the CPU (`ImageLoader`), not the texture. basisu is built from github.com/BinomialLLC/basis_universal (`cmake -G Ninja .. && ninja basisu`). Textures are disposed when a scene unmounts. `?debug=1` logs draw calls, triangles and texture bytes once a second (`PerfProbe`). The lower third's clock is throttled to 12 Hz (`useThrottledT`); scenes read `t` from the store inside `useFrame`, never through React.
- Scoreboard: `scripts/scoreboard.ts` runs every rocket × assumption set × window (90 missions, ~20 s) into `src/data/scoreboard.json`; `ui/Scoreboard.tsx` (lazy-loaded, `?board=1`) shows it with a preset switch, and a cell calls `flyMission(cfg, 'scoreboard')` (the pick step, preselected). **Re-run the script after any change to the sim or the vehicle data** — `src/sim/tests/scoreboard.test.ts` re-runs a cell per rocket and fails when the JSON is stale. `cellConfig`/`cellLabel` live in `ui/scoreboardTypes.ts`.
- Sharing: `ui/share.ts` puts the settings that differ from `DEFAULT_CONFIG` into `?mission=<base64url JSON>` (validated key by key on the way in); such a link opens on the intro's pick step with the mission described (`pickFrom: 'shared'` in the store) and the param is dropped on launch. The Share button on the verdict card uses the share sheet on touch devices, the clipboard elsewhere, and a toast (`showToast`). The link preview is `public/og.jpg` (1200×630), rendered by `node scripts/og-image.mjs [t]` from the built site in `?clean=1` mode (no overlays); the absolute og/twitter URLs are filled in at build time from `VERCEL_PROJECT_PRODUCTION_URL` (`__SITE_URL__` in index.html). Instrument Sans is self-hosted (`@fontsource/instrument-sans`, imported in main.tsx).
